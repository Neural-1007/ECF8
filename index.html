A Journey Through English

Keep scrolling to turn the pages. Each era is set in the type of its time.

c. 450 – 1100 AD

Old English

Germanic dialects brought to Britain by the Angles, Saxons and Jutes, later shaped by Old Norse after Viking settlement. Grammar was highly inflected and word order flexible.

Notable work: Beowulf, an epic poem in alliterative verse.

1 / 4
c. 1100 – 1500 AD

Middle English

After the Norman Conquest of 1066, Anglo-Norman French became the language of law and court, and thousands of French and Latin words entered English. Grammar simplified as noun endings faded.

Notable work: Chaucer’s The Canterbury Tales.

2 / 4
c. 1500 – 1800 AD

Early Modern English

The Great Vowel Shift changed pronunciation. Caxton’s press (1476) standardised spelling. Shakespeare coined hundreds of words and the King James Bible shaped the rhythm of English prose.

Notable work: Hamlet, King Lear, the King James Bible.

3 / 4
c. 1800 – today

Late Modern English

Industry and science drew thousands of terms from Greek and Latin. Empire and trade carried English worldwide, and after 1835 it entered Indian classrooms, giving rise to Indian writing in English: Tagore, Narayan, Roy, Ghosh.

Today: the working language of science, media and AI.
